## 2. The estimand ceiling — TEXTBOOK. Concede it.

**Plainly: yes, it is textbook, and it is not even an epidemiological result.** It is the
Fréchet–Hoeffding upper bound, restated as a bound on a dependence ratio. It follows in
one line and requires no causal assumption at all.

### 2a. The derivation, in the dissertation's own notation

§3.7 (`03_methods.md:194`) defines *rᵢ = aᵢ/a₀* as the fraction defence *i* passes, and
independence as *a₁₂ = r₁r₂a₀*. Writing *r₁₂ = a₁₂/a₀*:

> ρ* = a₁₂·a₀/(a₁a₂) = r₁₂/(r₁r₂)

That is exactly the **dependence ratio** of two binary events: τ = π₁₂/(π₁π₂). The
Fréchet–Hoeffding bounds for two binary marginals are

> max{π₁ + π₂ − 1, 0} ≤ π₁₂ ≤ min(π₁, π₂)

Divide through by π₁π₂:

> max{r₁ + r₂ − 1, 0}/(r₁r₂) ≤ ρ* ≤ min(r₁,r₂)/(r₁r₂) = **1/max(r₁, r₂)**

The upper bound the dissertation reports is the right-hand inequality. It is an identity
of probability, not a finding.

### 2b. What "monotonicity" is actually doing, and the one sentence that survives

This is the honest and slightly more interesting point, and it is the only part of the
ceiling analysis that is this study's own.

The Fréchet bound applies to a *conjunction of two events in one probability space*. The
study does not observe a conjunction: *a₁₂* is measured in a **separate composed arm**,
and a composed pipeline need not behave like the intersection of two blocking events (a
detector can change what the planner sees; a wrapper can change tool-call formatting).
The monotonicity assumption is therefore not doing the work the text implies — it is the
**bridge from the composed arm to the conjunction**, not the source of the bound.

**Defensible sentence, replacing the "methodological result" framing:**

> ρ* is a dependence ratio in the sense of Ekholm, Smith and McDonald (1995), and the
> Fréchet–Hoeffding bounds accordingly cap it at 1/max(r₁, r₂) whenever the composed cell
> behaves as the conjunction of its components' blocking events. The bound is standard;
> what is not standard is checking it against a pre-registered margin from measured
> pass-through rates before collection, and the assumption it rests on is not causal
> monotonicity but the substantive claim that the composed arm *is* that conjunction —
> which §4.II.b shows can fail outright.

### 2d. What must be conceded in the text

1. **§4.II.c's "The general form is reported in §5.2 as a methodological result"** must be
   demoted. A composition study checking its attainable ceiling against its margin before
   it runs is *good practice applying a standard bound*, not a methodological result.
2. **§1.6 output 3** claims "an operational definition of departure from multiplicative
   composition together with **the condition under which it is estimable at all**". The
   second clause is the bound. Attribute it in place.
3. **O4's acceptance criterion** ("with the binding condition stated in general form",
   `01_introduction.md:65`) is fine as a *deliverable*. It must not acquire a novelty
   claim in §5.2.
4. **A real arithmetic error, in two places.** §3.7 (`03_methods.md:210-211`) and §4.II.c
   both say a margin *m* "requires **some** component defence to pass less than 1/*m*".
   Since the ceiling is 1/max(r₁,r₂), exceeding *m* requires max(r₁,r₂) < 1/*m* — that is,
   **every** component, equivalently **the more permeable** component. As written the
   condition is necessary but not sufficient and is stated as though it were the
   requirement. Fix to "requires the more permeable component to pass less than 1/*m*".
5. **The lower bound is not reported.** Fréchet also gives ρ* ≥ max{r₁+r₂−1,0}/(r₁r₂).
   Reporting only the upper half of a two-sided classical bound invites the question.
   One sentence closes it.

### 2e. On the narrowing that landed while this ran

The withdrawal of "no pair in any suite reaches the margin" (banking reaching 3.06 at a
per-attempt unit) does not disturb the verdict — it sharpens the reason for it. The bound
is a function of the marginals alone, so it *must* move with the unit of analysis exactly
as r₁ and r₂ do. That is a property of the standard bound, which is another reason to
present it as standard: a bound that moves with the unit is unsurprising if it is Fréchet
and looks like instability if it is presented as a new result. Travel being uninformative
because all nine defences are byte-identical to undefended is consistent: r = 1 for every
defence gives a ceiling of exactly 1.000 by the same formula.

**Verdict on claim 2: the bound is DEAD as a contribution; the application NARROWS to a
statement about this study's own pair.** Defensible remainder:

> At the pass-through rates this study's own composable pair exhibits, the Fréchet ceiling
> on ρ* is at most 1.188 at every unit of analysis tried, against a pre-registered margin
> of 1.57. The confirmatory contrast could not have returned its supported verdict, and
> this was established from evidence in hand before compute was spent.

> model itself more robust**. Even if someone were to develop a technique that
> significantly enhanced a model's robustness to prompt injection, it would still be
> possible to obtain a stronger security argument through combining it with CaMeL."

This is the single most valuable citation available for claim 1 and it is currently absent
from the dissertation. The contrast is precise:

- The **§11** claim is careful and is *consistent with this study's finding*: defences that
  harden the model sit **beneath** the planner and do compose with CaMeL. That is exactly
  the representation axis.
- The **§6.3** claim is unqualified defence-in-depth advice and is what this study
  contradicts. A deployer following it with a prompt-level control or a harness-stage
  detector gets neither.

Framing the finding against the defence authors' own §6.3 recommendation converts it from
"layering guidance is wrong in general" — which is vague and contestable — into a specific,
citable, checkable contradiction of a named sentence in the paper that defines the axis.
It also pre-empts the obvious viva question ("did they not say it composes?").

### 3b. Duddu et al. (2411.09776) is nearer than audit 1 allowed

Fetched: `arxiv.org/html/2411.09776v2`, full text. Four overlaps, all verified:

1. **The thesis's headline sentence uses Duddu's vocabulary.** Their §1:
   *"Practitioners need effective combination techniques, either by **modifying existing
   defenses (invasive)**, or by identifying whether existing defenses can be combined
   **without modification (non-invasive)**."* The dissertation's "cannot be composed
   without modifying one of them" is the invasive/non-invasive distinction, uncredited at
   the point of use.
2. **Their conflict cause (ii) is this study's mechanism.** *"conflicts arise when … changes
   by an early-stage defense is overridden by a later-stage defense"*, formalised in the
   DefCon flowchart as global/local/none changes: *"Global changes by D₂ will override
   changes by D₁, thereby reducing its effectiveness."* A pipeline-owning defence making a
   global change that overrides a stage-inserting defence's local change is a textbook
   DefCon conflict.
3. **Their Table 1 is an "Overview of Pairwise Combinations among Defenses"** with an
   interaction type per cell — structurally the same artefact as the 36-of-91
   classification.
4. Their naïve baseline is **"mutually exclusive placement"** — stage-based reasoning about
   where defences sit.

**What still survives, and it is a real difference in kind.** Duddu's conflicts are
*effectiveness* conflicts among training-time and post-training defences on a single
classifier — adversarial training, DP-SGD, watermarking, group fairness, explanations.
DefCon predicts that a combination will be **less effective**. It has no category for a
defence that **never executes**, and none for a failure that is **silent** — every element
constructs, every fingerprint matches, and the configuration reports three defences.
Nothing in Duddu is agentic, none of it is prompt injection, and none of it concerns
pipeline architecture.

**Required:** cite Duddu at the point the claim is made, for the invasive/non-invasive
framing and the override mechanism — not only as a distant general antecedent in §1.2.
