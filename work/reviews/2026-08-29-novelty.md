### 1d. Published stacked-defence adaptive evaluation — **FOUND. Claim must narrow.**

> **arXiv:2604.23887** — Deep, P., Emmons, S., Fox, A., Bacon, K., McAllister, K.,
> Ortiz, P. and Flautner, K. (2026) 'Evaluation of Prompt Injection Defenses in Large
> Language Models'. v1 26 Apr 2026; v2 12 May 2026.

What it does, verified from the paper body:

- Nine configurations `t0`–`t8`. `t7` = **multi-layer, all defences combined**;
  `t8` = **multi-layer minus output filtering** (an explicit ablation). `t2` itself
  layers a regex stage beneath an LLM classifier.
- Adaptive attacker with three per-round phases (evolution / pattern-informed /
  exploration from a 40-strategy library), 25–500 rounds × 10 attacks, black-box.
- Result: layering without output filtering caps worst-case severity at 0.10 across
  5,000 attacks where every individual model-reliant defence reached ≥0.9; output
  filtering alone prevented all leaks across 15,000 attacks.

What it does **not** do, and this is where the contribution survives:

- **Not agentic.** The defender is a conversational "SecureBot" holding three secrets
  in its system prompt. No tools, no tool returns, no indirect prompt injection, no
  agent loop, no utility-under-attack measure.
- **Not factorial.** Two combined cells (all-on, all-on-minus-one) against six
  singles. It cannot estimate a pairwise interaction; there is no 2³ design.
- **No estimand.** Descriptive percentages, leak rates, max severity, rounds-to-
  critical-failure. No regression, no interaction term, no independence benchmark, no
  intervals, no significance test. Explicitly confirmed against the results section.

**Verdict: NARROW.** The following is defensible and the following only:

> "No adaptive evaluation of composed defences has been located in an agentic
> tool-use setting. The nearest precedent is Deep et al. (2026, arXiv:2604.23887),
> who stack prompt-level, sanitisation and filtering defences against an adaptive
> attacker, but do so on a single-turn conversational secret-extraction task rather
> than an agent reading untrusted tool output, and report the comparison as raw
> percentages against no independence benchmark. What is unmeasured is not that
> defences have never been combined, but whether a composed configuration departs
> from what independent failure predicts."

This paper is strictly stronger than TRYLOCK (Chen et al., 2026), which §2.7 currently
names as "the nearest precedent" on the grounds that it is *non*-adaptive. That
sentence is now wrong and must be rewritten around 2604.23887.

### 3b. RQ1 procedural determinacy — **NARROW, and a missing-citation finding**

§2.7 currently says: *"They are not assessed for procedural determinacy: no published
taxonomy reports whether two analysts applying it to the same deployment return the
same surface."*

As written that is a claim about enumerative taxonomies in general, and in that form
it is **false**. There is a fifteen-year empirical tradition in software engineering
of measuring exactly this for threat-modelling procedures, and none of it is cited
anywhere in the dissertation. All four verified against Crossref:

```
Scandariato, R., Wuyts, K. and Joosen, W. (2015) 'A descriptive study of Microsoft's
threat modeling technique', *Requirements Engineering*, 20(2), pp. 163-180.
https://doi.org/10.1007/s00766-013-0195-2.

Wuyts, K., Scandariato, R. and Joosen, W. (2014) 'Empirical evaluation of a
privacy-focused threat modeling methodology', *Journal of Systems and Software*, 96,
pp. 122-138. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jss.2014.05.075.

Tuma, K. and Scandariato, R. (2018) 'Two architectural threat analysis techniques
compared', in *Software Architecture (ECSA 2018)*, Lecture Notes in Computer Science.
Cham: Springer, pp. 347-363. https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-00761-4_23.

Van Landuyt, D. and Joosen, W. (2022) 'A descriptive study of assumptions in STRIDE
security threat modeling', *Software and Systems Modeling*, 21(6), pp. 2311-2328.
https://doi.org/10.1007/s10270-021-00941-7.
```

Van Landuyt & Joosen is the one that bites hardest: **96 STRIDE threat models of the
same system**, 640 assumptions analysed for how far the analysis varies between
analysts. Scandariato et al. and its replication (Mbaka & Tuma, arXiv:2208.01512;
arXiv:2208.01524) measure productivity, precision and recall of STRIDE across
analysts. Wuyts et al. does the same for LINDDUN.

**Does it duplicate our determinacy measure? No.** That literature scores analyst
output as **precision and recall against a researcher-constructed gold standard**,
plus descriptive variability. It does not report a chance-corrected inter-rater
coefficient over the elicited threat set, and it does not carry the answer-space /
chance-agreement control that §3 introduced because α is not comparable across
procedures with unequal category counts. Krippendorff's α over a derived property set,
with category count and chance agreement reported alongside precisely so the
comparison is legible, is not a published measure in this literature.

**Verdict: NARROW.** Two changes.

1. Scope the claim to agentic taxonomies. **Exact replacement sentence for
   `dissertation/02_literature_review.md:107`:**

   > "They are not assessed for procedural determinacy: no published *agentic* threat
   > taxonomy reports whether two analysts applying it to the same deployment return
   > the same surface. The software-engineering literature has asked this question of
   > general-purpose threat-modelling methods for over a decade — Scandariato et al.
   > (2015) and Wuyts et al. (2014) measure analyst-level precision and recall for
   > STRIDE and LINDDUN, and Van Landuyt and Joosen (2022) study ninety-six STRIDE
   > models of a single system — but it scores analysts against a researcher-built
   > gold standard rather than against one another, and it has not been asked of any
   > catalogue written for agentic systems."

2. Add the four references above to `06_references.md`. This converts a novelty risk
   into a strength: it shows RQ1 imports an established empirical-SE research design
   into a literature that has not used it, which is a better claim than "nobody has
   thought of this."

### 3c. Adjacent pre-emption risk for RQ3, found while sweeping

> **arXiv:2512.15081** — Moulton, R.H., O'Brien, A. and Hastings, J.D. (2025)
> 'Quantifying Return on Security Controls in LLM Systems'. 17 Dec 2025.

A decision-oriented framework that quantifies residual risk, converts adversarial
probe outcomes into financial risk estimates and return-on-control metrics, and
enables "monetary comparison of **layered defenses** for LLM-based systems", with

This does not pre-empt RQ3 — it has no ISO 31000 treatment mapping, no scientific or
engineering force, no adversary profile as an input, no adaptive attacker (Garak

measurement uncertainty into a control-deployment decision for layered LLM defences,
which puts §3.8's line — *"which no published framework in this area currently
reports"* — under strain.

**Suggested narrowing for §3.8:** from "which no published framework in this area
currently reports" to "which no published agentic-security decision framework reports;
the nearest, Moulton et al. (2025), propagates probe uncertainty into expected loss
but does not report whether the resulting treatment decision is invariant."

Also relevant to RQ3 and uncited: **arXiv:2604.07551** 'MCP-DPT: A Defense-Placement
Taxonomy and Coverage Analysis for Model Context Protocol Security' (8 Apr 2026) —
a defence-placement taxonomy with coverage analysis. Adjacent to §2.3's four-axis
taxonomy. Not a pre-emption; §2.3's axes are distinguished by guarantee character
(probabilistic vs deterministic), which is the load-bearing cut.
