# External examiner review of the simulated end-state — 29 August 2026

Reviewer stance: external examiner, cyber security academic. Not a supervisor, no
investment in the work. Read against the standard a programme committee at USENIX
Security / CCS / NDSS would apply, and against the module's own artefact aim.

Nothing in `dissertation/` or `preregistration/` was edited by this review.

---

## 0. Headline judgement

The study is methodologically more careful than most MSc work in this area and more
careful than several published papers it cites. The pre-registration, the
four-verdict partition, the equivalence branch, the internal-baseline decision, the
AutoDojo duplication finding and the §3.4a withdrawal paragraph are all genuinely
good. The forbidden-claims register is clean; nothing has crept back.

**But the simulated end-state does not support the claim the study is built to make,
and three of the defects that cause this are in the pre-registered analysis rather
than in the run.** They are fixable now, for free, before any GPU is rented. After
the run they are not fixable at all.

The three that matter most, in order:

1. **The equivalence margin is anchored to a composed rate that no contrast in the
   design has.** ρ* = 1.57 is derived from an independence prediction of 0.175. The
   design's own expected pass-throughs give 0.308, 0.084, 0.058 and 0.046 for the
   four contrasts. The consequence tie — the thing that makes the margin defensible
   rather than conventional — holds for none of them, and is anti-conservative by a
   factor of 2 to 4 on the three that contain the deterministic axis.
2. **"Clusters are not the binding constraint" is an artefact of the comparison
   used to establish it.** The power script compares 49 vs 98 clusters at the same
   *total* n, i.e. it re-slices the same data into smaller clusters. Held at equal
   observations per cluster, clusters bind about as hard as n does.
3. **The dry run's utility gate is not the pre-registered utility gate**, and
   CHECKPOINTS finding #3 — one of the four headline findings — is generated by
   the discrepancy.

Details and derivations below.

## 1. Threat-model realism — a different adversary, not an upper bound

§3.5 grants the attacker (a) a clean per-query success signal and (b) 25 adaptive
probes, and calls the combination "an upper bound on attacker capability… a
measurement of worst-case rather than expected-case composition."

**That framing is wrong in both directions at once, and a reviewer will say so.**

**The oracle is stronger than worst case.** Per §3.6, attack success is "the
injection's goal state is reached in the environment, scored by the benchmark's own
checker." The optimiser is therefore reading *environment ground truth*, not
observable side effects. No adversary has this. For exfiltration payloads the real
attacker's signal is close (they receive the data); for state-changing payloads —
transfers, deletions, calendar writes — it is often entirely unobservable. So the
oracle's realism varies *across injection tasks within the study*. That is a
measurable confound and the design currently ignores it.

*Cheap fix, and it is a contribution rather than a patch:* classify each of the 49
injection tasks by whether its goal state is observable to an external attacker, and
report ρ* split on that classification. The observable-goal subset is the realistic-
oracle estimate; the rest is the upper bound. This costs nothing at run time (it is
a post-hoc stratification of data already collected) and it converts the study's
weakest assumption into a reported result. Do this.

**The budget is weaker than worst case.** 5 rounds × 5 variants = 25 probes. The
nearest precedent, Deep et al. (2026), runs 25–500 rounds × 10 attacks. Nasr et al.
run far more. §3.4a concedes the budget "fell… because the factorial buys
combinations with the same compute" and that it "is the change most likely to bias a
result toward the study's own refutation branch." So the document says in one place
that the attacker is unrealistically strong and in another that it is
budget-constrained and biased toward the null. Both cannot be foregrounded. The
honest description is: **a small budget with an unrealistically clean feedback
signal.** Write it that way.

The supporting argument that "twenty-five adaptive probes would themselves be an
indicator of compromise" does not survive contact. Twenty-five probes spread across
victims and time is not detectable by anything. If anything the IoC argument shows
the budget is *too small* to be the thing a detection layer would catch.

**What is missing entirely is a write primitive.** The threat model never states how
the attacker gets content into the tool return, whether one payload must work across
many victims (transferability) or can be tailored per victim, or who controls the
channel. The design implicitly assumes per-victim tailoring with a ground-truth
oracle — the rarest configuration in the wild. A security venue will ask for one
paragraph on this and it is not there.

**One further exposure.** SCOPE.md documents that the optimiser's `defense_filtered`
failure category is detected "by checking whether the injection's ground-truth
parameters survived into the tool result." That is again privileged information. The
attacker is not merely attack-aware; on that channel it is partially white-box on
the *evaluation harness*. §3.5's careful tier-3/tier-4 boundary argument does not
cover it. Expect the question.

## 2. Configuration realism — what is the result *of*?

**Nothing shipped looks like this.** A production tool-using agent in 2026 runs a
frontier model behind a vendor guard (Prompt Shields / Bedrock Guardrails / Llama
Guard), a tool allowlist, human confirmation for consequential actions, and egress
controls. The study composes spotlighting + PIGuard + CaMeL over Llama-3-8B.

The scoping to "inline mitigations" (§1.4) excludes human-in-the-loop confirmation
and egress control. **Those are the layers deployers actually rely on for
defence-in-depth.** So the study tests layering minus the layers that carry the
practical argument. That sentence belongs in §5.3 verbatim; the current limitation
("Detection-and-response layers, rate limiting and alerting are… outside what this
study varies") understates it by framing the omission as monitoring rather than as
mitigation.

**CaMeL with an 8B quarantined model is not CaMeL, and the deviation biases the
hypothesis.** The prereg (§3) and §3.2 both declare it and say the instance is
"weaker here than as published, which moves both its single-axis rate and every
composed rate containing it, and not by amounts that cancel." That is an
acknowledgement, not an analysis. Work the sign through:

- CaMeL's utility and much of its residual attack success depend on the quarantined
  model parsing untrusted data into typed values. An 8B model will fail parses.
- Failed parses lower a_C for reasons that are *quarantine incompetence*, not
  information-flow control.
- a_C enters the denominator of ρ* for SxC, PxC and SxPxC. **A depressed a_C lowers
  the independence prediction and therefore mechanically raises ρ* for the same
  composed rate.**

So the single declared deviation from a published configuration pushes three of four
confirmatory contrasts toward "supported". This is the most dangerous unforced error
in the design and the document does not currently state its direction. It must, and
it should be paired with a mitigation: **run one C-cell with a stronger hosted
quarantined model.** That is one cell, hosted, cheap, and it converts an
un-signed confound into a bounded sensitivity.

**Where the configuration is defensible.** As a study of *mechanism axes* rather
than of shipped stacks, the selection is right and the probabilistic/deterministic
cut in §2.3 is the load-bearing one. Keep the claim at the axis level and it holds.
The moment the claim becomes "layered defences admit more attack success than
guidance assumes," it is a claim about stacks, and this configuration is not one.

## 3. Utility — what a security architect does on Monday

**Given the simulated end-state: nothing.** I want to be precise about why, because
the reason is arithmetic and it is fixable.

Recomputing the absolute departures from the design's own expected pass-through
rates (S=0.80, P=0.55, C=0.15, a₀=0.70):

| contrast | independence predicts | simulated observed | Δ* (pp) | ρ* needed for a 10pp departure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SxP | 0.308 | 0.595 | **+28.7** | 1.32 |
| SxC | 0.084 | 0.180 | +9.6 | 2.19 |
| PxC | 0.058 | 0.113 | +5.5 | 2.73 |
| SxPxC | 0.046 | 0.099 | +5.3 | 3.16 |

Read the last column. **A single ratio margin of 1.57 corresponds to 17.6pp on SxP
and 2.6pp on the triple.** §3.7 justifies the margin as "the departure that would
change the treatment the decision rule of §3.4 returns," derived from the scientific
force's 10pp cut-point at an independence rate of 0.175. No contrast in this design
has an independence rate of 0.175. The consequence tie holds nowhere.

The consequence for the simulated result is direct: of the two contrasts declared
SUPPORTED, one (SxP) is decision-relevant at 28.7pp; the other (SxC) is 9.6pp,
*below* the 10pp cut-point that defined the margin. The two undetermined contrasts
would still be below the cut-point even if they resolved. **So the study can return
"SUPPORTED" on a departure its own decision rule would grade as no effect.** A
hostile reviewer will find this in ten minutes and it is fatal to the headline.

And SxP — the only contrast with a real departure — is spotlighting plus a
classifier: the two cheap probabilistic layers. No architect is choosing between
those. The decision that costs money is whether to fund a CaMeL-style rearchitecture,
and on that question every contrast returns undetermined.

**What would change the answer, in order of cost:**

1. **Free.** Make Δ* co-primary and set the consequence margin *per contrast* at
   that contrast's own independence prediction. Pre-commit that "supported" requires
   both the ratio interval above the margin *and* Δ* above 10pp. This is the single
   highest-value change available and it must happen before the lock.
2. **Free.** Report the finding in the units a practitioner uses: expected
   compromised sessions per 10,000 tasks under the stack, against what the component
   figures predict, with an interval. "Your residual is 5pp higher than the vendor
   arithmetic implies" is actionable. "ρ* = 2.2" is not.
3. **Free, and I think it is the real contribution.** Promote CHECKPOINTS finding #2
   to the headline: *composition estimates are not obtainable at the deterministic
   axis at any budget a security team could field.* That says vendor claims about
   layered stacks are not empirically checkable with current tooling. It is true
   whichever way the numbers fall, it does not depend on the triple resolving, and
   it is a finding about evidence availability that a practitioner can act on
   (specifically: do not accept a composed-effectiveness claim, because nobody can
   verify one).
4. **~one cell.** A frontier-model transfer probe (see §4).
5. **~one axis.** A shipped-style layer (tool allowlist or confirmation gate) as a
   fourth factor, so the result speaks to a stack someone runs.

## 4. The 8B target — press hard, and there is a cheap answer

Three distinct transfer problems, only one of which the document addresses:

1. **Defence effectiveness is model-dependent.** Spotlighting works by getting the
   model to honour delimiters — precisely the instruction-following fidelity an 8B
   model is worst at. r_S on Llama-3-8B is plausibly *not* r_S on a frontier model,
   and the direction is not obviously monotone.
2. **The attacker/target capability mismatch is not one that exists in
   deployment.** A frontier optimiser attacking an 8B target inflates a₀ and every
   pass-through relative to a realistic pairing.
3. **The study cites its own counter-evidence and does not apply it.** §3.3 rejects
   the white-box regime partly because Hofer et al. (2026) report "attacks optimised
   on smaller open-weight models fail to transfer to frontier models." That result
   is used to justify not doing something, and is then not applied to the study's own
   generalisation. In a viva I would read that sentence back and ask why transfer
   failure is decisive against the white-box arm but silent on the target model.

§5.3 and the prereg both declare the one-family limitation honestly, which counts
for a great deal. But declaring a limitation is not the same as bounding it.

**The cheap answer, and I would expect the candidate to propose it unprompted:** ρ*
is a *ratio*, and ratios may transfer where rates do not. That is a hypothesis, and
it is testable for roughly the cost of one contrast — run SxP only (no camel, so no
quarantine problem, no floor effect, and it is the contrast the design can actually
resolve) against a hosted frontier model at reduced n, and check the sign and rough
magnitude. Given a total budget of ~$265 / 346 GPU-hours, this is the highest-value
marginal spend in the entire study. It converts "a result about Llama-3-8B" into "a
result about Llama-3-8B with one transfer probe across a capability gap." Do it.

## 5. Statistical defensibility under adversarial reading

### 5.1 The margin does not do what §3.7 says it does

Covered in §3 above. This is the most serious analytic defect and it is
pre-registered, so it is only fixable before the lock.

Second-order point on the same paragraph: §3.7 already concedes the tie is
rate-dependent ("at a₀ = 0.70 the margin is ten points of attack success, at the
adequacy floor of a₀ = 0.40 under six"). The author has *seen* the dependence on a₀
and not noticed the much larger dependence on the composed baseline, which varies by
a factor of nearly seven across the four contrasts.

### 5.2 "Clusters are not the binding constraint" is an artefact of the comparison

`power_sim.py` establishes this by comparing `power(2.00, 49, n_per_cell=200)` with
`power(2.00, 98, n_per_cell=200)` — the same total sample re-sliced into twice as
many, half-sized clusters (4.1 → 2.0 observations per cluster). That is not doubling
the clusters; it is fragmenting the data.

Re-running with observations per cluster held fixed:

| configuration | obs/cluster | power at ρ* = 2.00 |
|---|---|---|
| 49 clusters, n=200 (reported) | 4.1 | 0.18 |
| 98 clusters, n=200 (reported) | 2.0 | 0.20 |
| **49 clusters, n=400** | **8.2** | **0.34** |
| **98 clusters, n=800** | **8.2** | **0.54** |

Clusters bind about as hard as n does. The claim in §3.7 and in
`work/w2-composition/power/RESULTS.md` — "Tests per cell bind, not clusters" — is
not supported by the simulation offered as its evidence, and it is load-bearing: it
is the stated justification for the 800-per-cell allocation and for saying the three
extra suites buy nothing on precision. Recompute before locking; the allocation may
change.

### 5.3 The power table is computed at an operating point three of four contrasts do not occupy

`power_sim.power()` defaults to `r1=r2=0.5`. The design expects r_C = 0.15. So the
tabulated power describes SxP tolerably and **does not describe SxC, PxC or SxPxC at
all**. The dry run is itself the evidence: at a true ρ* of 2.25 the tabulated power
is 0.91, and the simulation resolves 2 of 4. The prereg's honest-sounding "the
confirmatory arm is powered only for ρ* ≥ 2.25" is *optimistic* for three quarters
of the confirmatory family. Recompute power per contrast at its own r_i and report
four rows, not one. This costs nothing and it is exactly the kind of thing that
looks like concealment if it is discovered after the fact rather than declared
before.

### 5.4 The utility gate in the dry run is not the pre-registered gate

Prereg §7: `U₁₂ ≥ 0.30·U₀`. With U₀ = 0.62 that is a floor of **0.186**.
`dry_run.py:224-225` tests `u_pred >= UTIL_FLOOR`, i.e. against **0.30** absolute.
The line also contains dead arithmetic
(`UTIL_FLOOR * UTIL[""] / UTIL[""] * UTIL_FLOOR / UTIL_FLOOR`, which evaluates to
`UTIL_FLOOR`) — a sign the gate was written and never re-read.

Consequence for CHECKPOINTS finding #3, which is one of the four headline findings:

| camel benign utility | U_SPC | prereg gate (0.186) | dry-run gate (0.30) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.42 | 0.349 | PASS | PASS |
| 0.30 | 0.249 | **PASS** | **FAILS** |
| 0.20 | 0.166 | FAILS | FAILS |

The middle row is the one the finding rests on. **Under the pre-registered gate the
triple survives; camel's utility would have to fall below about 0.22 to exclude it.**
The finding as written ("the triple cell is at real risk of exclusion by its own
utility gate") is substantially an artefact of a gate the pre-registration does not
contain.

Worse, the *informative* limb cannot fire in the simulation at all. `utility()`
returns exactly the independent prediction, so the ratio u₁₂ / (u₁u₂) is 1.0 by
construction and the limb the prereg calls "the limb that catches super-multiplicative
competence collapse" is untestable. That is the limb most likely to fire in reality,
given Li and Zhao's 47–77% step-one failure. The dry run therefore does not model
the risk it claims to have found, and does not model the risk that actually exists.

Fix both, then regenerate CHECKPOINTS. Finding #3 may survive, may invert, or may
need restating as "the gate has never been exercised against a realistic
super-multiplicative collapse."

### 5.5 The simulated truth is not the estimand (non-collapsibility)

`true_rates()` applies ρ* on the *marginal* probability scale; `simulate()` then adds
a shared cluster random effect on the *logit* scale (SD 0.8). Logit is
non-collapsible, so the marginal ρ* the estimator targets is not the nominal ρ* the
DGP was parameterised with. Measured over 200k draws:

| nominal ρ* | induced marginal ρ* (all four contrasts) |
|---|---|
| 1.00 | 0.975 – 1.011 |
| 2.25 | 2.062 – 2.070 |

An ~8% attenuation at the alternative, so the power table's row labels are wrong by
about that much (conservatively — real power at a true marginal 2.25 is higher than
tabulated). Small in magnitude, but it means the stipulated truth and the estimated
quantity are different parameters, and a statistically literate reviewer will find
it. Either simulate on the marginal scale, or solve for the conditional parameter
that induces the target marginal ρ*.

### 5.6 Table 4.x as simulated is unauditable

The table reports composed rate, ρ*, CI and verdict. It does not report a₀, a₁, a₂,
the independence prediction, Δ*, the residual, the cluster count, U_c, or which
verdict state applied. **A reader cannot recompute ρ* from it** — and indeed cannot:
from the stated composed rates and the design's nominal pass-throughs one obtains
1.93 / 2.14 / 1.95 / 2.14, not the tabulated 2.24 / 2.15 / 2.01 / 2.22, because the
realised a₀ and singles differ from nominal and are not shown. Every composition
table in Chapter 4 must carry all four rates, the independence prediction, Δ*, and
the utility retention. This is a five-minute change and it is the difference between
a table a reviewer can check and one they must trust.

### 5.7 The verdict asymmetry, and a prior nobody holds

Compounding §5.1: the design can declare "supported" but can almost never declare
"refuted" (§3.7 concedes the equivalence branch is attainable "only when the observed
ratio sits close to one"), and pre-commits everything else to "undetermined." Now add
the antecedents. Knight and Leveson (1986) refuted independence for N-version
programming forty years ago; He et al. (2017) refuted it for combined adversarial-
example defences. **The null is a hypothesis nobody in either literature believes.**

A design that (a) tests a null no one holds, (b) is powered only for large
departures, (c) can support but rarely refute, and (d) uses a margin that does not
track consequence, is a design whose "supported" verdict carries little information
and whose "undetermined" verdict carries none. The informational content sits
entirely in the *magnitude* — Δ* — which is precisely the quantity the margin fails
to govern. §5.1 and §5.7 are the same defect seen from two directions, and fixing
§5.1 fixes both.

### 5.8 Sample and clustering — broadly defensible

49 injection tasks as clustering units, CR2 with Satterthwaite plus a wild cluster
bootstrap, wild bootstrap reported where they disagree: correct, and better than most
of the literature. The unit-of-analysis argument in §3.7 (injection task, not attempt,
because the optimiser stops on success and would censor differentially by defence
strength) is genuinely sharp and I would credit it explicitly. The 800/cell
allocation needs revisiting in light of §5.2 but the inferential machinery is sound.

## 6. Priority and novelty — the narrowing is just enough, with conditions

The forbidden-claims register is clean. I checked all seven patterns against current
`dissertation/` and `preregistration/` text and agree with the novelty audit: nothing
has crept back, X5 is now structurally impossible (two regimes), and §3.4a's CaMeL
withdrawal paragraph is a model of how a withdrawn claim should read. AutoDojo is
foregrounded in §3.2, §3.4a and §2.6 — the antecedent is properly credited, which is
the thing I was most prepared to fail this on and did not.

Remaining exposure, in order of how badly it goes:

**He et al. (2017) — the same hypothesis with the same answer.** Currently cited in
§1.2 in a single clause. If ρ* comes back supported, the finding is "the 2017
adversarial-examples result also holds for agentic pipelines." That is a legitimate
contribution — new setting, new mechanism class — but the dissertation frames the
question as newly opened ("the corresponding open question"), and a reviewer will
say the answer was known before the study started. **The defensible distinction is
the estimand and it must be made explicitly and early:** He et al. showed a combined
defence *can be broken*; they did not quantify departure from independence, so their
result cannot tell a deployer whether the stack is worth building. That is the
sentence, and it should be in §1.2 and repeated in Methods, not left implicit.

**Deep et al. (2026) — narrowing to "agentic" holds, but the contrast needs
engaging.** §2.7's rewrite is good and correctly bounded. One live problem: Deep et
al. report layering *working*, and attribute the effect to **output filtering**,
which this study does not include as a factor. If this study reports layering
failing, the first reviewer question is why the layer that worked in the nearest
precedent was excluded. §3.2's placement sensitivity (detector on quarantined-model
output) is adjacent but is a one-arm sensitivity check, not a factor. Either promote
it or address the asymmetry explicitly in Part IV.

**Knight and Leveson (1986)** is cited correctly in §3.7 and is an asset. See §5.7
for the way it also cuts against the design.

**One claim the audit did not flag.** §1.6 output 3 claims "a per-axis ranking of
defence cost-efficiency under adaptive attack." With one instance per axis, an axis
ranking is an instance ranking wearing an axis label — and §3.7 already concedes
"no within-axis term is estimable." Withdraw or restate as per-instance. This is
exactly the kind of small overclaim that a viva uses to open a wider line.

**Pre-emption watch.** The audit's identification of `yooky0ng/agentdojo-defense-suite`
as the live threat to the engineering contribution (C1) is right, and the
15 September re-check should happen.

## 7. The artefact question

The module aim — "Analyse, design and implement a software solution to a
well-defined problem" — applies to this candidate regardless of programme, and
**the work meets it.** The composition layer (`compose.py`, `run_cell.py`), the
derivation instrument (`work/w1-surface/instrument/`), the fingerprinting tool that
produced the AutoDojo integrity finding, the power and dry-run simulators, and the
`checks/` suite together constitute a substantial, purposeful and original software
artefact. The composition layer in particular is a real engineering contribution:
the harness genuinely cannot compose defences, and the fingerprint construction gate
is the right design response to the silent-failure risk.

The exposure is that the headline artefact has so far produced two single-cell smoke
runs (`g0_travel_none.json`, `g0_travel_spotlighting.json`) and nothing at scale. If
the factorial does not run, §3.9's contingency becomes the actual report and the
artefact is untested at the scale it was built for. That is a schedule risk, not a
design defect, but it should be visible to the board.

## 8. Ten viva questions, hardest first

The three I believe are **currently unanswerable from the document** are marked
**[UNANSWERABLE]**.

**Q1. [UNANSWERABLE] Your margin of 1.57 is derived from an independence prediction
of 0.175. What is the independence prediction for the SxPxC contrast, and what
absolute departure does ρ* = 1.57 correspond to there?**
*A good answer needs:* 0.046, and 2.6pp — against a scientific-force cut-point of
10pp that the margin was derived from. Then a concession that a single ratio margin
cannot be consequence-tied across contrasts whose baselines differ sevenfold, and a
proposal (per-contrast Δ* margin as co-primary).
*Currently supported?* No. §3.7 acknowledges rate-dependence only through a₀ and
treats the composed baseline as fixed at 0.175. The document has no answer.

**Q2. [UNANSWERABLE] You serve CaMeL's quarantined model as Llama-3-8B rather than
the GPT-class model its authors used. Which way does that bias ρ*, and by how much?**
*A good answer needs:* the sign worked through — a weaker quarantined model depresses
a_C, a_C is in the denominator, so ρ* rises for SxC, PxC and SxPxC. Acknowledgement
that the study's one declared deviation pushes three of four contrasts toward its own
hypothesis. Then a bound: one C-cell with a stronger hosted quarantined model.
*Currently supported?* No. Both §3.2 and prereg §3 declare the deviation and say it
"moves both its single-axis rate and every composed rate containing it, and not by
amounts that cancel" — which is a statement that the direction is unknown, offered
where the direction is knowable and unfavourable.

**Q3. [UNANSWERABLE] A security architect reads your abstract on Monday. Two of four
contrasts supported at ρ* ≈ 2.2. What do they do differently?**
*A good answer needs:* honesty that for the two resolved contrasts the actionable
content is thin, that the only decision-relevant departure is between the two cheap
probabilistic layers, and that the money question — whether to fund a CaMeL-style
rearchitecture — returns undetermined. Then the pivot to the finding that *is*
actionable: composed-effectiveness claims are not empirically checkable at the
deterministic axis, so do not accept one.
*Currently supported?* No. §5.6 is a `<!-- WRITE AFTER PART II -->` comment, and §1.4
and §5.2 both imply a practitioner-facing conclusion the numbers will not carry.

**Q4. Your power analysis says clusters are not the binding constraint. You showed
that by comparing 49 and 98 clusters at n = 200. What happened to observations per
cluster?**
*A good answer needs:* it halved, from 4.1 to 2.0; the comparison fragments a fixed
sample rather than adding data; held at equal obs/cluster, doubling clusters moves
power at ρ* = 2.00 from 0.34 to 0.54. Then: the 800/cell allocation and the
"extra suites buy breadth not precision" justification both need revisiting.
*Currently supported?* Partly — the code is there and the candidate can be walked to
it, but the written claim in §3.7 and in `power/RESULTS.md` is stated the wrong way
and would have to be conceded.

**Q5. Your attacker reads the benchmark's ground-truth checker for its per-query
signal. Which real adversary has that, and does it vary across your 49 injection
tasks?**
*A good answer needs:* concession that none does; that exfiltration payloads
approximate it and state-changing payloads do not; and the proposal to stratify ρ* by
goal observability as a realism analysis obtainable post hoc for free.
*Currently supported?* Partially. §3.5 identifies the assumption clearly and
honestly, which is more than most papers do, but frames it as a uniform upper bound
and does not notice the within-design variation.

**Q6. He et al. (2017) asked whether combining weak defences yields a strong one and
answered no, with an adaptive adversary and three combined defences. What is left for
you to find?**
*A good answer needs:* the setting (pipeline over untrusted tool output vs classifier
over images) and — the load-bearing one — the estimand: they showed a stack *can be
broken*, not by how much it departs from what its components predict, which is what a
deployer needs to size residual risk.
*Currently supported?* Yes, just. §1.2 carries the clause. It should be sharper and
should recur in Methods.

**Q7. Your power table assumes each defence passes 0.50. Your dry run assumes camel
passes 0.15. Which contrasts is the table valid for?**
*A good answer needs:* SxP only; the other three are less powered than tabulated;
the dry run's 2-of-4 resolution at a true ρ* of 2.25 against a tabulated 0.91 is the
evidence. Then: recompute per contrast before locking.
*Currently supported?* Partly. The numbers are in the repository; the inconsistency
is not stated anywhere in the dissertation or the pre-registration.

**Q8. CHECKPOINTS finding #3 says the triple is at real risk of exclusion by its own
utility gate at a camel utility of 0.30. Apply the pre-registered gate to that case.**
*A good answer needs:* U_SPC = 0.249 against a floor of 0.30 × 0.62 = 0.186, so it
passes; the dry run compares against 0.30 absolute; the finding is substantially an
artefact; and the second limb (u₁₂₃ ≥ 0.75·u₁u₂u₃) cannot fire in the simulation
because `utility()` returns the independent prediction by construction.
*Currently supported?* No, and this one is uncomfortable because the finding is
presented as "a pre-committed finding" rather than as a bug.

**Q9. Deployment guidance recommends layering, but the layers deployers ship are
human confirmation on consequential actions and egress control. You scope both out.
What does your result say to them?**
*A good answer needs:* honest restriction of the claim to inline mechanism axes, and
recognition that the excluded layers are the ones carrying the practical
defence-in-depth argument. Ideally: an argument for why the axis-level result is
still informative about the inline portion of a real stack.
*Currently supported?* Weakly. §1.4 scopes to inline mitigations in one sentence and
§5.3 does not revisit it. The framing ("Detection-and-response layers, rate limiting
and alerting") reads as excluding monitoring, when what is excluded is mitigation.

**Q10. You cite Hofer et al. as evidence that attacks optimised on small open-weight
models fail to transfer to frontier models, and use it to reject the white-box arm.
Why does your composition result transfer?**
*A good answer needs:* the ratio-vs-rate argument (ρ* may transfer where a₀ does
not) offered as a hypothesis rather than a claim, plus a concrete cheap test — one
frontier-model SxP contrast at reduced n as a sign check.
*Currently supported?* Partly. §5.3 and prereg §7 declare the one-family limitation
squarely and refuse to approximate cross-arm consistency, which is creditable. The
inconsistency with §3.3's use of Hofer et al. is not addressed anywhere.
