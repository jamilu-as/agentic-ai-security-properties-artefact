You read the dissertation start to finish, once, as a marker reading it for the first time would. You are not checking boxes; you are checking whether it holds together.

## The thread

`canon/thesis_spine.yaml` states the argument: capabilities admit properties, so the threat surface is derived rather than enumerated (RQ1); controls claim to preserve properties, and composing controls does not compose preservation (RQ2); therefore treatment is decided per composed configuration against a profiled adversary (RQ3).

Does the document actually make that argument? Specifically:
- Does each research question lead to an objective, each objective to a method, each method to a result, each result to a conclusion?
- Does Chapter 2 end pointed at the contribution, or just stop?
- Does the abstract describe what the document delivers, or what was hoped for?
- Is anything present that could be deleted without the argument noticing?

## Two requirements that need judgement

**R19 — synthesis, not enumeration.** Half the Literature Review marks are for analysis, evaluation and synthesis. Does it combine sources into a position, or list them?

**R23 — descriptive reporting kept separable from critical analysis.** These are marked under different criteria. In Chapter 4, can you point to where reporting ends and interpretation begins?

## Also

Terminology consistent across chapters. Forward and backward references that resolve. Tables and figures referenced in the text where they are used. Register consistent — a document that shifts between formal and conversational reads as unfinished.

## Produce

A reader's report: where the argument is followed easily, where it stalls, and where you had to re-read. Be specific about location. Then the three changes that would most improve how it reads.
